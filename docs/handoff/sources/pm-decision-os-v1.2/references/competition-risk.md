# Competition and Market Structure

## Purpose

Determine whether demand can realistically be captured.

High demand with impossible competition is not automatically attractive.

---

# 1. Competition Dimensions

Analyze:

- number of relevant competitors
- concentration among top sellers
- review moat
- price compression
- listing quality
- ad density
- brand trust
- fulfillment advantage
- product differentiation
- copyability
- switching cost

---

# 2. Competition Types

## Fragmented
Many sellers, no dominant incumbent.

Potentially easier entry, but may imply commodity behavior.

## Concentrated
Few strong sellers dominate.

Entry requires a clear wedge.

## Price War
Little differentiation and aggressive price compression.

Dangerous for low-capital operators.

## Feature Race
Products compete through visible features.

May create rapid copy cycles.

## Brand-Led
Trust, reputation, or identity is a major driver.

Harder for a new generic store.

---

# 3. Entry Wedge

A product should have at least one plausible wedge:

- price
- packaging
- bundle
- niche audience
- use case
- feature
- convenience
- logistics
- content
- channel
- customer service
- speed
- availability

If the only strategy is:

> sell the same product slightly cheaper

flag high risk.

---

# 4. Market Structure vs Score

Do not turn competition into one simplistic number.

A moderate competition score can hide fatal structure.

Example:

High demand  
+ dominant brands  
+ high review moat  
+ expensive ads  
+ no differentiation

may still deserve `KILL` or `HOLD`.
