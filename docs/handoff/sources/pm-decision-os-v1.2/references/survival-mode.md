# Survival Mode

## Purpose

Use when the business must survive before it can optimize.

Typical contexts:

- Small budget
- Marketplace / e-commerce
- Product selection
- Early-stage operation
- Limited data
- Limited inventory budget
- High uncertainty
- Need for fast cash recovery

Primary principle:

> Survival before optimization.

Secondary principle:

> Reduce the cost of being wrong.

---

# 1. Priority Order

In Survival Mode prioritize:

1. Capital preservation
2. Contribution margin
3. Fast feedback
4. Low inventory exposure
5. Small reversible bets
6. Simple operations
7. Short cash cycle
8. Cheap validation
9. Repeatable supply
10. Scale after evidence

Do not prioritize brand completeness over survival.

---

# 2. Product Selection Flow

Candidate Product  
↓  
Hard Kill Filter  
↓  
Demand Signal  
↓  
Unit Economics  
↓  
Supply / Logistics Risk  
↓  
Failure Cost  
↓  
Cheap Validation  
↓  
Small Launch  
↓  
Real Data  
↓  
Scale / Improve / Kill

---

# 3. Hard Kill Filters

Possible kill criteria:

- Contribution margin below minimum
- Product fragile / costly to ship
- High return probability
- Legal / compliance uncertainty
- MOQ too large
- Inventory locks too much cash
- Supply unstable
- High defect risk
- Price competition destroys margin
- Requires brand trust that does not yet exist
- Validation cost is too high
- Operational complexity exceeds capability

---

# 4. Survival Scoring Dimensions

For surviving candidates, consider:

## Demand
Is there evidence people want it?

## Margin
Can it make money after full costs?

## Competition
Can we realistically compete?

## Supply
Is sourcing stable?

## Logistics
Is fulfillment simple?

## Returns
Is return risk manageable?

## Capital
How much cash is locked?

## Validation Cost
How cheaply can this be tested?

## Failure Cost
How much is lost if wrong?

## Cash Recovery
How quickly can capital return?

## Scalability
Can a winner be expanded?

---

# 5. Survival Preference

Prefer:

Good-enough upside  
+ Low downside  
+ Fast learning  
+ Repeatable supply  
+ Short cash cycle

over:

Huge theoretical upside  
+ High inventory risk  
+ Weak evidence  
+ Long payback

---

# 6. Product Selection Decision

For each product classify:

## TEST
Low-cost test justified.

## SMALL BET
Small inventory / limited launch justified.

## HOLD
Potential exists but key uncertainty remains too large.

## KILL
Fails survival constraints.

## SCALE
Real data proves healthy economics and repeatability.

---

# 7. Scale Criteria

Scale only when enough are true:

- Real orders exist
- Contribution margin is positive
- CAC is acceptable
- Return rate is controlled
- Supply is stable
- Inventory turns are acceptable
- Customer complaints are manageable
- Reorder / repeat signals are positive where relevant
- Operational load is sustainable

---

# 8. Survival Review

At each cycle ask:

- How much cash is at risk?
- How fast can we learn?
- What would make this fail?
- What data will change the decision?
- Can we test without buying full inventory?
- If wrong, how much do we lose?
- If right, can we scale?
