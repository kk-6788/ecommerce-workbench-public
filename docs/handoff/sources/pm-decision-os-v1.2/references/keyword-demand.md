# Keyword and Demand Analysis

## Purpose

Use keyword data as evidence of demand intent, not as a standalone product score.

Keywords can help answer:

- Is demand present?
- How large is the demand pool?
- What intent clusters exist?
- Is demand generic or product-specific?
- Is the market seasonal?
- Are multiple keywords describing the same demand?

---

# 1. Build a Full Intent Pool

Do not rely on one headline keyword.

Create a keyword pool including:

- primary product term
- synonyms
- use-case terms
- problem terms
- material / feature terms
- audience terms
- size / format terms
- long-tail purchase-intent terms

Example structure:

Product:
`dog cooling vest`

Intent clusters:

- generic product
- hot-weather use
- outdoor / hiking
- breed / size
- cooling function
- summer safety

---

# 2. Deduplicate Demand

A major risk is double counting.

Examples:

`dog cooling vest`
`cooling vest for dogs`
`pet cooling vest`

may represent overlapping demand.

Do not sum all search volume as if each were unique.

Use:

- intent clusters
- semantic deduplication
- overlap adjustment
- conservative aggregation

---

# 3. Keyword Quality

Assess each keyword by:

## Relevance
Does it represent the exact product?

## Intent
Informational, comparison, or purchase intent?

## Specificity
Does it point to a clear product need?

## Stability
Is volume stable or event-driven?

## Seasonality
Does demand collapse outside a narrow window?

## Competition
How difficult is the term to win?

---

# 4. Demand Layers

Separate:

## Discovery Demand
Broad category interest.

## Problem Demand
Searches describing pain or need.

## Product Demand
Searches for a product type.

## Purchase Demand
High-intent commercial queries.

Purchase-intent evidence should generally receive more weight than broad informational interest.

---

# 5. Cross-Signal Validation

Do not use keyword data alone.

Combine with:

- marketplace result counts
- sales estimates
- review velocity
- price distribution
- ad density
- social discussions
- transaction data
- conversion data after test launch

The stronger the decision, the more independent signals should agree.

---

# 6. Common Errors

Avoid:

- adding overlapping keyword volume
- treating all terms as equal intent
- using global volume for a local market
- ignoring seasonality
- confusing trend spikes with stable demand
- using irrelevant broad terms to inflate demand
- scoring demand without confidence

---

# 7. Output

For each candidate summarize:

- Total keyword pool
- Intent clusters
- Estimated unique demand
- Purchase-intent strength
- Seasonality
- Competition signal
- Confidence
- Key caveat
