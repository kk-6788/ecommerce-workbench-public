# Metrics

## Objective

Measure whether users receive value and the business improves.

Do not treat activity as success.

---

# 1. Metric Layers

## North Star Metric

Represents recurring delivery of core user value.

It should not be selected only because it grows.

---

## Outcome Metrics

Examples:

- Contribution margin
- Gross profit
- Revenue
- Orders
- Retention
- Active customers
- Repeat purchase

---

## Leading Indicators

Examples:

- CTR
- Product-detail-view rate
- Add-to-cart rate
- Signup rate
- Activation rate
- Conversion rate

Leading indicators predict outcomes but do not replace them.

---

## Quality Metrics

Examples:

- Return rate
- Defect rate
- Complaint rate
- Negative review rate
- Cancellation rate

---

## Efficiency Metrics

Examples:

- CAC
- ROAS
- Cost per activation
- Inventory turnover
- Fulfillment cost
- Support cost

---

## Guardrail Metrics

Prevent harmful optimization.

Example:

Increase orders while:
- keeping return rate below threshold
- maintaining contribution margin
- avoiding abnormal complaint growth

---

# 2. Metric Tree

Build:

Business Goal  
↓  
Outcome Metric  
↓  
Leading Drivers  
↓  
Operational Metrics

Example:

Contribution Margin  
↓  
Orders × Margin per Order  
↓  
Traffic × Conversion × AOV × Margin Rate  
↓  
CTR / Add-to-cart / CAC / Refund rate

---

# 3. Experiment Metrics

Every experiment should define:

- Primary metric
- Secondary diagnostic metric
- Guardrail metric
- Success threshold
- Failure threshold

Do not decide success after seeing results.

---

# 4. Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Revenue-only thinking
- Optimizing CTR with low-quality traffic
- Increasing sales while margin collapses
- Treating one-time purchase as retention
- Using too many KPIs without hierarchy
- Changing success criteria after launch
